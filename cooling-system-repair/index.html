Protect the engine

Do not keep driving an overheating vehicle

If the temperature warning is active or steam is visible, pull over safely and shut the engine off. Never open a hot cooling system; pressurized coolant can cause severe burns. Tell us whether the gauge rose suddenly, whether coolant is visible under the vehicle, and if the heater or cabin temperature changed. Avoid restarting or driving until safe next steps are discussed.

Cooling concerns can involve leaks, fans, circulation, sensors, or other causes. The engine must be cool and safely accessible for inspection. Some repairs require extended disassembly or equipment unsuitable for mobile service, so the location and scope need confirmation before a visit is arranged.

Useful observations

Help describe the cooling issue

Temperature pattern

Note whether overheating occurs at idle, in traffic, or during a drive and whether a warning appeared.

Coolant evidence

Describe puddles, residue, odor, or how often the coolant level has needed attention. Do not touch hot components.

Related warnings

If a dash light is involved, see automotive diagnostics for assessment information.

Arrange a safe assessment

Confirm the location and next step

  1. Call with the current condition. Share vehicle details, temperature warnings, leaks, and whether it is safely parked.
  2. Discuss suitability. Confirm location, travel, safe access, availability, and what can be assessed without running a hot engine.
  3. Review options. Agree on the diagnostic scope and estimate; additional repair work requires separate discussion and approval.

Never drive an overheating vehicle to an appointment without first discussing safety. Square booking terms and any fee or deposit should be reviewed before confirmation.
