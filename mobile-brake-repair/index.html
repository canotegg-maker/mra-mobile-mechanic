What you notice matters

Describe the change in braking

Include whether a sound occurs at low or high speed, if it changes with light or firm pedal pressure, whether the vehicle pulls, and if the pedal feels soft, unusually hard, or pulses. A brake warning light or visible fluid leak is also important context. Do not assume a noise means pads alone; inspection determines what components and follow-up are appropriate.

If braking feels unsafe, avoid driving the vehicle and discuss safe next steps when you call. Brake service may require a level, stable parking surface and room to work. M.R.A. confirms job scope, location, travel, and appointment timing individually; some conditions may need a different repair setting.

Before scheduling

Prepare the vehicle details

Noise and pedal symptoms

Note when noises began and whether braking performance or pedal travel has changed.

Vehicle and access

Share year, make, model, exact location, and whether the vehicle can be positioned safely.

Related warning signs

If a dashboard light or electrical symptom is present, an initial diagnostic assessment may help clarify the issue.

Service steps

Assessment before repair approval

  1. Contact M.R.A. Explain the brake symptoms and share vehicle and location details.
  2. Confirm suitability. Discuss safe access, mobile work scope, travel, availability, and the estimate.
  3. Review the inspection. Consider the findings and approve an agreed repair scope before work begins.

No parts or repair outcome should be presumed from symptoms alone. Review any fee or deposit shown in the Square booking terms before confirming.
