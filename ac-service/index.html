Understand the cooling change

Cold air depends on more than refrigerant

Note whether the air is warm at all vents or only some, whether cooling changes at idle or while moving, how airflow compares with before, and whether you hear a click, squeal, or rattle. Mention recent A/C work and any warning lights. Weak cooling may involve airflow, controls, electrical components, leaks, or other system conditions, so the right test comes first.

A/C systems use pressurized refrigerant and require appropriate service procedures and equipment. Do not vent refrigerant or add products based on a guess. M.R.A. will explain whether the requested assessment can be handled at your location and what service options are suitable; not every repair is a mobile job.

Describe the symptom

Observations that help scope an A/C visit

Temperature change

Explain how long cooling takes and whether the air becomes warmer in traffic or at idle.

Airflow and controls

Report weak airflow, vents that do not change direction, or a fan that behaves differently.

Unusual sounds

Note when clicking, rattling, or belt-like noises occur and whether they change with A/C selection.

Plan the assessment

Confirm system needs before booking

  1. Share vehicle details. Send year, make, model, location, and a description of temperature, airflow, and sounds.
  2. Confirm mobile scope. Discuss the tests and equipment needed, travel, location access, and appointment availability.
  3. Review findings and estimate. Agree on any follow-up repair or parts before work is authorized.

Service availability and suitability depend on the vehicle and location. Review Square booking terms, including any fee or deposit, before confirming.
